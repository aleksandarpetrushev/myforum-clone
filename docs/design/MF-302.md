# MF-302 Survive the programme-publish rush

Status: targets agreed, test design open
Author: Aleksandar Petrushev
Date: 2026-11-13

## Question

The Annual Meeting programme manager: "When the programme goes live at 09:00, about 3,000 participants open My Forum and book sessions within ten minutes. Will it hold?" The ticket asks for a load test that models the rush, a written answer with numbers (what breaks first, at what request rate), and at most two changes ranked by cost, the cheaper one implemented and measured.

## The morning, from the programme manager

- Programme email at 09:00 CET to about 3,000 participants; most arrive in the first 10 minutes, the first 2–3 minutes are the worst.
- Each participant browses, opens sessions, checks recommendations, and books 4 to 6 sessions.
- About 20 popular sessions (keynotes, big panels) fill within minutes and spill into waitlists. Most sessions hold 50–200 people; keynotes hold 1,000.
- What matters most: nobody is told the wrong thing about a seat. Then speed. Then confirmations within a minute.

## Targets (agreed by the engineer and the programme manager, 2026-11-13)

| Measure | Target |
|---|---|
| Arrival model | 2,000 participants in the first 2 minutes, 1,000 more over the next 8; about 25 requests each; 70% of bookings go to the 20 hot sessions |
| Rates this implies | Bookings 83/s at the peak. All requests about 420/s at the peak, bursts about 800/s |
| Registration (`POST /api/registrations`, waitlist join) | p95 ≤ 1.2 s, p99 ≤ 3.5 s |
| Page views (discovery, session page, My registrations, recommendations) | p95 ≤ 500 ms, p99 ≤ 1,500 ms |
| Errors | 5xx and timeouts ≤ 0.1% of requests. `event_full`, `seats_available` and other 4xx answers the app gives on purpose are not errors |
| Wrong outcomes | 0: no `reserved_spots` above capacity, no `reserved_spots` that differs from the confirmed and offered count, no participant with two rows for one event, no 201 without a row. Checked in the database after the run |
| Notification lag (outbox `created_at` to published) | p95 ≤ 60 s, max ≤ 120 s (the stall alert pages at 120 s) |
| Personalization memory | Under 70% of its limit throughout (MF-204 postmortem, P1) |

Rejected along the way: an error budget of 0.001% (fewer than one failed request in the whole morning, which no test of this size can show, and stricter than the programme manager asked), and 5 requests per participant (counts the bookings and leaves out the browsing around them).

## Known risk before measuring

`Events::Register` locks the event row (`FOR UPDATE`) for every registration. All bookings for one keynote queue on one row, so registration p99 on hot sessions is the likely first failure, before page views. The test must concentrate bookings on hot sessions or it will miss this.

## What exists

`bin/rails bench:traffic` (`backend/lib/bench/traffic.rb`) starts production-mode Puma over TLS against `my_forum_benchmark`, offers a fixed arrival rate of discovery GETs as three personas, and reports p50/p95/p99 per scenario. It does not register anyone, does not run Sidekiq or the Go service, and has no ramp. Best measured so far: 300 requests/s of discovery with 2 Puma workers, p95 37 ms (`docs/benchmarks.md`).
