# MF-302 Survive the programme-publish rush

Status: fix A implemented, waiting to be measured
Author: Aleksandar Petrushev
Date: 2026-11-13

## Question

The Annual Meeting programme manager: "When the programme goes live at 09:00, about 3,000 participants open My Forum and book sessions within ten minutes. Will it hold?" The ticket asks for a load test that models the rush, a written answer with numbers (what breaks first, at what request rate), and at most two changes ranked by cost, the cheaper one implemented and measured.

## The morning, from the programme manager

- Programme email at 09:00 CET to about 3,000 participants; most arrive in the first 10 minutes, the first 2–3 minutes are the worst.
- Each participant browses, opens sessions, checks recommendations, and books 4 to 6 sessions.
- About 20 popular sessions (keynotes, big panels) fill within minutes and spill into waitlists. Most sessions hold 50–200 people; keynotes hold 1,000.
- What matters most: nobody is told the wrong thing about a seat. Then speed. Then confirmations within a minute.

## Targets (agreed by the engineer and the programme manager, 2026-11-13)

| Measure | Target |
|---|---|
| Arrival model | 2,000 participants in the first 2 minutes, 1,000 more over the next 8; about 25 requests each; 70% of bookings go to the 20 hot sessions |
| Rates this implies | Bookings 83/s at the peak. All requests about 420/s at the peak, bursts about 800/s |
| Registration (`POST /api/registrations`, waitlist join) | p95 ≤ 1.2 s, p99 ≤ 3.5 s |
| Page views (discovery, session page, My registrations, recommendations) | p95 ≤ 500 ms, p99 ≤ 1,500 ms |
| Errors | 5xx and timeouts ≤ 0.1% of requests. `event_full`, `seats_available` and other 4xx answers the app gives on purpose are not errors |
| Wrong outcomes | 0: no `reserved_spots` above capacity, no `reserved_spots` that differs from the confirmed and offered count, no participant with two rows for one event, no 201 without a row. Checked in the database after the run |
| Notification lag (outbox `created_at` to published) | p95 ≤ 60 s, max ≤ 120 s (the stall alert pages at 120 s) |
| Personalization memory | Under 70% of its limit throughout (MF-204 postmortem, P1) |

Rejected along the way: an error budget of 0.001% (fewer than one failed request in the whole morning, which no test of this size can show, and stricter than the programme manager asked), and 5 requests per participant (counts the bookings and leaves out the browsing around them).

## Known risk before measuring

`Events::Register` locks the event row (`FOR UPDATE`) for every registration. All bookings for one keynote queue on one row, so registration p99 on hot sessions is the likely first failure, before page views. The test must concentrate bookings on hot sessions or it will miss this.

## What exists

`bin/rails bench:traffic` (`backend/lib/bench/traffic.rb`) starts production-mode Puma over TLS against `my_forum_benchmark`, offers a fixed arrival rate of discovery GETs as three personas, and reports p50/p95/p99 per scenario. It does not register anyone, does not run Sidekiq or the Go service, and has no ramp. Best measured so far: 300 requests/s of discovery with 2 Puma workers, p95 37 ms (`docs/benchmarks.md`).

### What breaks first

1. **Notifications, already at 1×.** Requests are answered in milliseconds, but confirmations reach participants about five minutes late, and the 120 s stall alert would page on-call in the middle of the rush. The cause is the outbox poller (`Outbox::Publisher`, `ProcessOutboxJob`). One chain handles at most 100 rows per pass, one at a time: each row is locked, two jobs are enqueued, Go is called over HTTP synchronously and the row is updated. The job then waits 1 s before the next pass even when rows are waiting. That is about 40 rows a second. Bookings arrive at 60–80 a second in the first three minutes (12,521 confirmations in the measured run), so the backlog grows until arrivals slow, and drains only after the rush.
2. **Web requests at about 2× (around 1,000 requests a second at the peak) on this machine** (*assumed*), with 2 Puma workers × 5 threads. Registrations for the five keynotes queue on their event row lock (`Events::Register`, `FOR UPDATE`) and are the slowest requests. This is outside the agreed morning; on a deployed fleet with more workers it moves further out.

Seat correctness held at every scale: no oversold session, no lost or duplicate booking, no seat-count drift.

### Changes, ranked by cost (the ticket allows two)

| | Change | Cost | Expected effect (*assumed*) | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | The poller does not wait when a pass was full: `ProcessOutboxJob` reschedules at once when it published a full batch, and the batch grows to 500 rows | About half a day, mostly tests | About 65 rows a second. 1×: lag p95 41 s, max 88 s, **passes**. 1.5×: p95 190 s, still fails | Low. Same chain, same order and failure handling (MF-204); the poller simply stops idling |
| B | Take the Go call off the poller: the poller only enqueues, and delivery to Go runs in parallel jobs, serialized per ordering key (event, or user's topic follows) so MF-204's ordering still holds | 2–3 days, needs a design note | Several hundred rows a second; lag under 15 s at 2× | Medium. Ordering per key must be kept outside the poller, and the per-row failure isolation moves with it |

Recommendation: implement A now (it meets the agreed morning) and measure; keep B as the plan for anything beyond 1×.

**B: won't do unless more than 3,000 participants are expected** (engineer, 2026-11-15). The programme manager's page asks them to tell us at least two weeks before programme day if they expect more.

Answer for the programme manager: [Programme morning: will My Forum hold?](https://claude.ai/artifact/L31jLudPvUmCpA21VGg5rf)

**A is implemented** (branch `mf-302-outbox-throughput`, 2026-11-15): `Outbox::Publisher::BATCH_SIZE` is 500 and `ProcessOutboxJob.wait_after` enqueues the next pass at once after a full batch that published rows. It enqueues directly rather than with `wait: 0`, because Sidekiq polls scheduled jobs only every few seconds, which also stretched the old 1 s wait. **Its effect is not measured yet.** Run `bin/rails bench:rush` at 1× and 1.5× and replace the assumed lag numbers above before telling the programme manager the morning holds.
